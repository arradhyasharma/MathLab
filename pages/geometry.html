The angle-sum property

G—01

The sum of the three interior angles of every triangle is always 180°.

Change the angles in the experiment below. Check whether they can form a triangle.

Remember A triangle must have three positive interior angles whose sum is exactly 180°.

03 / CONCEPT

Area and perimeter

G—02

Area measures the surface covered by a shape. Perimeter measures the total distance around its boundary.

For a triangle, area is half the product of its base and perpendicular height.

Remember Area is measured in square units. Perimeter is measured in ordinary units.
TRIANGLE AREA
½ × b × h
b = base, h = perpendicular height

05 / CONCEPT

Heron's formula

G—03

Heron's formula calculates the area of a triangle when all three side lengths are known.

First calculate the semi-perimeter, then use it to find the area.

Remember The three side lengths must satisfy the triangle inequality.
HERON'S FORMULA
√[s(s−a)(s−b)(s−c)]
s = (a + b + c) ÷ 2

07 / CONCEPT

Equilateral triangles

G—04

An equilateral triangle has three equal sides and three equal interior angles.

Each angle measures 60°. Its area and height can be calculated directly from its side length.

Remember All three sides are equal, and all three interior angles are equal.
EQUILATERAL TRIANGLE
A = (√3/4)a²
Height = (√3/2)a · Perimeter = 3a

05 / CONCEPT

Understanding quadrilaterals

G—03

A quadrilateral is a closed polygon with four sides and four interior angles.

Squares, rectangles, parallelograms, rhombuses and trapeziums are examples of quadrilaterals.

Remember The sum of the interior angles of a quadrilateral is always 360°.
QUADRILATERAL PROPERTY
360°
Sum of interior angles
